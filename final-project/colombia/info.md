BACKGROUND OF THE STATISTICAL OPERATION

The periodic production and publication of foreign trade data in Colombia began in 1916. Between 1916 and 1951, these statistics were compiled by the General Directorate of Statistics, an entity attached to the Office of the Comptroller General of the Republic.

From 1951 until August 1953, these statistics were compiled by the National Directorate of Statistics, which was attached to the Office of the President of the Republic. From that point on, the DANE—which replaced the aforementioned Directorate—assumed the responsibilities for producing and disseminating these statistics.

Since 2005, the DIAN has provided the DANE with a monthly file containing export declarations, which the DANE then validates and verifies. Furthermore, the country’s regional customs offices submit paper-based (lithographic) export declarations—those completed manually—to the DANE, which then carries out the processes of receipt, batching, review, and data entry, in order to consolidate the information for subsequent release.

INTERNATIONAL STANDARDS


Export statistics are produced in accordance with international recommendations issued by the UN and the General Secretariat of the Andean Community (CAN).
The most significant of these are as follows:

- Separately recording certain goods for which data allows for adjustments to international merchandise trade totals, in order to consolidate information for National Accounts and the Balance of Payments, and to compile detailed foreign trade statistics.

- Initially using the customs territory as the statistical territory.

- In line with international recommendations, the long-term objective is for the statistical territory to correspond to the economic territory.

- Foreign merchandise trade statistics must record all goods that are added to or subtracted from a country’s stock of material resources as a result of their entry (imports) into or exit (exports) from its economic territory. Goods that are transported through the country, or that are temporarily admitted to or removed from it, do not increase or decrease the country’s stock of material resources and, therefore, are not included in international merchandise trade statistics.

- It should be noted that Article 4 of CAN Decision 511 stipulates that the statistical territory of member countries shall be defined as their customs territory, pending the adoption of provisions that would allow for the compilation of statistics based on the economic territory; it is under this principle that foreign trade statistics in Colombia have been produced.

In adopting these recommendations, the methodology incorporates export modality codes to identify which goods are to be included in or excluded from the statistics. Similarly, exports of gold for monetary use, as well as banknotes and coins in circulation—which are not identified by internal codes—are excluded.

Summary
Export statistics record the legal outflow of goods to another country or to a Colombian free trade zone; they are based on export declarations submitted to the country’s various customs authorities.

These exports are statistically recorded once customs has finalized the export declaration. Based on this, the information is presented using specific export codes—established by DANE and the Colombian National Tax and Customs Directorate (DIAN)—to determine which goods should be included in or excluded from foreign trade statistics, in accordance with international recommendations issued by the Andean Community (CAN) and the United Nations (UN).

It is essential to have regular access to these statistics, as they reflect the trends and patterns of such activities nationwide—including their contribution to the Gross Domestic Product (GDP), their role in job creation, and the volume of goods moving through these channels.

In this context, this statistical operation aims to meet the needs and requirements of individuals, institutions, and both national and international organizations engaged in research related to the sale of goods abroad.

Data Type
Statistical operation based on administrative records (admin records)
Unit of Analysis
The export declaration (DEX) serves as the raw material for compiling export statistics—excluding statistics on petroleum and its derivatives—meaning that the export declaration acts as both the unit of analysis and the unit of observation. For the study of exports of petroleum and its derivatives, the necessary information is provided to DANE on a monthly basis by the companies that export these products.

Study Notes
NEED FOR INFORMATION

Exports consist of goods that leave a country and are recorded by customs authorities. They are valued at FOB (Free On Board) prices, as this includes all costs associated with transporting the goods to the customs border, export duties, and the cost of loading the goods onto the chosen mode of transport. Their importance lies in tracking the trends of exportable goods; they also serve as an input for calculating a country’s balance of trade with the rest of the world.

VARIABLES

Classification variables: destination country, department of origin of the merchandise, department from which the merchandise is dispatched, mode of transport, flag of the transport vessel/vehicle, and tariff subheading.

Analysis variables: tariff subheading, gross weight (in kilograms), net weight (in kilograms), commercial unit of measurement, quantity of units, FOB value in US dollars, and FOB value in local currency (pesos).

## DATA COLLECTION

Once the export declarations (DEX) have been submitted to the DANE central office by the DIAN (National Tax and Customs Directorate) and companies exporting petroleum and its derivatives, they are organized and grouped by customs office and type of initial declaration. These documents are subsequently batched, reviewed for quality/consistency, and entered into the system.

Receipt control is managed through specific forms, which allow for the classification—by customs office and petroleum-exporting company—of the status regarding the submission (or non-submission) of paper-based (lithographic) declarations and petroleum reports.

Furthermore, it is possible to track the number of paper-based declarations submitted, as well as the number of records received. This information is subsequently cross-checked against the reports generated during the data capture process.

In the event that neither paper-based declarations nor reports concerning the export of petroleum and its derivatives are received, then on the first business day following the deadline for submission to DANE, emails must be sent to the relevant customs offices and companies. These communications should request either the missing information or an official letter/email confirming that no paper-based declarations were processed and no exports of petroleum or its derivatives took place during the month in question.

Classification is performed by customs office, according to the type of declaration:

- Initial declaration: Submitted to the customs office for the first time during the data reference month.

- Correction declaration: Submitted and delivered to the customs administration jurisdiction in the following scenarios: voluntarily, upon authorization from the General Directorate of Foreign Trade (under the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism), or due to exceptional circumstances. - Declaration amendment: This applies only when the declarant changes the export type from temporary to definitive—specifically in cases involving temporary exports intended for re-importation in the same condition, or temporary exports for passive inward processing (outward processing).

When submitting paper-based (lithographic) export declarations via email, customs offices must include an official letter (memorandum) listing the export declaration numbers sent to DANE, as well as the period to which the documents pertain.

This official letter, along with the paper-based export declarations (DEX), must also be sent in physical format to the DANE central office.

Once the paper-based export declarations (DEX) for the relevant period have been classified and registered, a formal request is issued to any customs offices that submitted incomplete or missing information, or declarations that do not pertain to DANE. This process includes the following:

- Requesting any export declarations (DEX) that were not submitted by the customs office, as indicated in the official letter or email.

- Notifying the relevant customs office if field 38, "Type of declaration," has not been completed on a paper-based export declaration (DEX).

- Identifying and returning any standard export declarations that are not designated as the "DANE copy."

- Requesting any declarations that are missing supplementary pages or contain incomplete information.

SYSTEM DESIGN

Export statistics are generated using data from electronic export declarations (DEX), which are processed via a OneDrive folder. This database is provided to DANE by DIAN on a monthly basis. Additionally, data from paper-based (lithographic) export declarations—submitted monthly to DANE by customs offices—are validated and entered into the system. These are combined with direct reports on petroleum and its derivatives (provided by major exporting companies) to create a unified database.

Subsequently, an export database with the file extension ".AVA" is created. This database contains records from the paper-based export declarations, the direct reports on petroleum and its derivatives, and the file downloaded from DIAN.

Any corrections or adjustments pertaining to previous periods—received during the current reference period—are incorporated into the relevant historical databases, ensuring that these datasets remain up-to-date with the latest revisions.

The databases are stored in a dedicated research folder on one of the institution’s servers. Access to this folder is restricted to the technical research team and specific internal users from other research projects who have been granted "read-only" access.

Regarding the anonymization process, it should be noted that the highest level of data disaggregation provided in the results is at the tariff subheading level. Due to statistical confidentiality requirements, DANE does not publish information that could identify individual exporters, such as their tax identification number (NIT) or legal business name.

## TRAINING

Following a unified set of criteria and the guidelines established by DANE, the personnel participating in the research receive training that ensures the appropriate application of concepts and the proper production of high-quality statistical information.

The training process generally consists of:

- Basic concepts regarding foreign trade statistics, based on the United Nations manual on concepts and definitions for international merchandise trade statistics.

- Methodological aspects of the research.

- The statistical information production process: data acquisition, validation, contextual analysis, and the generation of output tables.

- Training on the specific functions of the role.

To evaluate the extent to which the operation’s objectives—regarding coverage, the information provided, and the quality level of operational processes—are met, it is necessary to calculate a reliability indicator. This indicator allows for the monitoring of the aforementioned tasks, which are carried out by the Collection and Aggregation Directorate (DRA) team at DANE Central.

The reliability indicator is calculated as a simple average of all indicators applied to the various production phases that comprise the statistical operation (coverage, data review, data capture, and data validation). DATA CONSOLIDATION

The research team (DRA) is responsible for consolidating data from across the country. Using the SAS software, the team performs validation procedures and corrects any inconsistencies.

Once the databases have been consolidated, output tables are generated, focusing on the key variables under study. This data is then analyzed by comparing it with figures from the same month of the previous year, as well as from preceding months, to identify any inconsistencies, ensure that the established methodology has been applied correctly, and—within the national context—explain export dynamics for that period.

STANDARDS, SPECIFICATIONS, AND RULES FOR VALIDATION, CONSISTENCY, AND IMPUTATION

Data derived from export declarations undergo a series of validation checks and must meet specific criteria. These checks cover variables such as the department code, destination country, shipment dates, transaction type code, tariff subheading, and the value, quantity, and volume of the exported products.

For these variables, the data is checked for appropriate length or format; for instance, the gross weight (in kilograms) must be greater than the net weight (in kilograms). If this condition is not met, an inconsistency is flagged. Consequently, each field is subject to specific requirements and defined corrective actions.

Nomenclatures and Classifications Used
These refer to the systems used to classify goods, based on levels of disaggregation, usage characteristics or economic purpose, and the geographic zones or economic groups with which such goods are associated.
For the production of export statistics, DANE currently uses the following classifications:
Customs Tariff (10 digits): The tariff currently in effect in Colombia is based on the Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System—an internationally agreed-upon framework adopted under the name "NANDINA Nomenclature." It classifies goods according to the nature of their components and is presented at the following levels of disaggregation:
• Item: Consists of ten digits and represents the highest level of disaggregation within the nomenclature.
• Section: Composed of the first four digits; it encompasses a set of chapters containing goods with similar physical or chemical compositions.
• Chapter: Composed of the first two digits; it covers goods whose specifications allow them to perform similar functions or satisfy similar needs.
Standard International Trade Classification, Revision 3 (SITC Rev. 3): The SITC facilitates the compilation of international trade statistics for all goods subject to foreign trade and promotes the international comparability of these statistics. The SITC groupings reflect, on one hand, the materials used in production, the stage of processing, market practices, and product uses; and on the other, the significance of the goods in terms of global trade and technological changes (see Annex B). Central Product Classification Adapted for Colombia (CPC Rev. 2.0 A.C.): This classification was developed by the UN and adapted by DANE starting in 2013. Its primary purpose is to provide a general framework for the international comparison of statistics related to goods and services, presenting data by product type. A key feature of the CPC—as a general-purpose classification—is that it provides more detailed information than other sector-specific classification systems (see Annex C).
Furthermore, the CPC enhances harmonization across various sectors of economic statistics and strengthens the role of National Accounts as a coordination tool for such statistics.
SITC (Standard International Trade Classification) Revision 2, Adapted by Embedded Technological Intensity: This classification groups goods according to their embedded technological intensity. It includes primary goods and manufactured goods, among others (see Annex D).
International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC Rev. 4, A.C.): This is the international reference classification for economic activities, which can be used for the collection and presentation of statistical reports based on those activities (see Annex E).
